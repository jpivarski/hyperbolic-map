Infinite Circle Limit III

The same fish, but tiled properly. One octagon’s worth of art is stored (escher-atlas.json, 84 shapes) and the callback returns that same tile for every tile coordinate. Scroll as far as you like: the pattern never runs out, which is what Escher was reaching for and could not physically carve.

Why {8,3}

Circle Limit III sits on the {8,3} tiling — regular octagons, three per vertex — with the chiral 433 symmetry group: four fish to an octagon, with 4-fold rotation centres at the octagon centres. Repeating one tile everywhere only produces a consistent pattern if the art is invariant under the tile’s stabiliser, and under 433 that stabiliser is C4, not C8. Eight-fold art would give a different member of the family, not Escher’s.

That also rules out the two obvious ways to walk the tiling: the 8-fold rotation about a tile centre and the half-turn about a shared edge midpoint are both outside 433 (which has no order-2 points at all). The library therefore uses 3-fold rotations about alternate vertices — which is exactly Escher’s own “connect alternate vertices” construction.

Honest about the seams: Escher’s woodcut is hand-drawn and the 2012 tracing of it was approximate — its author wrote that “some fish don’t line up in orientation or color” and that he gave up a few layers out. The art here is re-anchored onto an octagon centre and symmetrised to exact C4, but it still does not meet the octagon boundary perfectly, so you will see seams where tiles abut. Turning off clipping above trades the hard cuts for soft overlaps. The tiling is exact; the art is a documented approximation.
