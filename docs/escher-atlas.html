Infinite Circle Limit III

The same fish, but tiled properly. One octagon’s worth of art is stored (escher-atlas.json) and the callback returns that same tile for every tile coordinate. Scroll as far as you like: the pattern never runs out, which is what Escher was reaching for and could not physically carve.

The tile is traced directly from the woodcut and made exactly C4-symmetric by construction — one 90° sector is traced and repeated by exact rotation. That is not decoration: a tile’s frame is only defined up to the stabiliser C4, so art that is not C4-invariant visibly rotates as you scroll across a tile boundary. See the tiling diagnostics, where you can switch between art that obeys the rule and art that breaks it.

Why {8,3}

Circle Limit III sits on the {8,3} tiling — regular octagons, three per vertex — with the chiral 433 symmetry group: four fish to an octagon, with 4-fold rotation centres at the octagon centres. Repeating one tile everywhere only produces a consistent pattern if the art is invariant under the tile’s stabiliser, and under 433 that stabiliser is C4, not C8. Eight-fold art would give a different member of the family, not Escher’s.

That also rules out the two obvious ways to walk the tiling: the 8-fold rotation about a tile centre and the half-turn about a shared edge midpoint are both outside 433 (which has no order-2 points at all). The library therefore uses 3-fold rotations about alternate vertices — which is exactly Escher’s own “connect alternate vertices” construction.

Why the fish are not Escher’s four colours

Because they provably cannot be. Around an octagon centre Escher’s four fish alternate green–orange–green–orange, so his colouring is only C2 while the shape is C4. Painting C4 shapes in four different colours fails the stabiliser rule outright — the library’s own check scores it 0.36 — and the fish would change colour under you as you scrolled.

So the four fish in an octagon share a colour, and the variety comes from the tile class instead. The 433 group admits a three-class homomorphism, which gives a proper 3-colouring of the octagons in which no two neighbours match. Being a homomorphism, it is the same whichever route the walk took to reach a tile — unlike a tile’s word address, which is not canonical and would flicker.

Honest about the source: Escher’s woodcut is hand-drawn, and the raster it is traced from here is 316 px across, so the outlines are approximate and the fish are simplified. What is exact is the geometry and the symmetry: the disk radius, centre and tiling rotation were fitted by requiring the pattern to be invariant under the walk group’s own generator, and the tile is C4-symmetric to 4 × 10−17. The fitted numbers are recorded in the tile’s own meta block.
