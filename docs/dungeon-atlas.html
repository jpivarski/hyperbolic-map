Infinite dungeon

The same dungeon, split into an atlas. Every cell of the tiling is its own little coordinate system, so the numbers describing a room are always small no matter how far from the origin that room is. Wander as far as you like — or zoom out until the whole infinite world fits in the disk, where a world-turtle is carrying it.

Three sources, composed in one callback

The callback that supplies each tile merges three things, which is the point of returning data rather than a URL:

  1. a prototype room — seven shapes, stored once, drawn in every cell;
  2. video-game characters — finitely many, so they live in a lookup keyed by cell coordinate;
  3. room numbers — there are infinitely many, so they are computed on the fly from the cell’s own coordinates.

The turtle and the star field are not library features. They are two image layers defined in demo/layers.js: one turns with the disk, one does not. Turning with the disk means something slightly different here than in a single-patch view: the view matrix is expressed in the anchor tile’s frame, and the anchor changes as you walk, so the raw rotation jumps by as much as 34° at a cell boundary that the dungeon itself crosses perfectly smoothly. The layer cancels those jumps. What it cannot do is pin the shell rigidly to the plane — that needs the global frame this whole page exists to avoid.

Clipping is off here, deliberately. The 2012 room art is drawn straddling cell boundaries — the floor plate spans the corner where four cells meet, and the doors are what connect adjacent rooms, so they have to cross the boundary. Each piece is still drawn exactly once, by the cell that owns it, so nothing overlaps; but confining each cell’s drawing to its own cell severs the junctions and leaves fragments. Tick the box above to see it happen. The Escher tile, whose art is designed to fill its tile, clips correctly — which is why the library makes this a per-atlas choice rather than a rule.

Why the numbers are always small: cell (latitude, longitude) is x ∈ [lon·2lat, (lon+1)·2lat], y ∈ [2lat, 2lat+1] in the half-plane — but in its own frame every cell is the identical box x ∈ ±1/(2√2), y ∈ [2−½, 2½]. The 2lat divides out. In the single-patch version the far rooms need coordinates up to 11712, where a disk coordinate has only about seven significant digits left.
