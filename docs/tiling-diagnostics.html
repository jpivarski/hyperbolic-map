Tiling diagnostics

THE RULE this page exists to demonstrate. A tile’s frame is defined only up to the tile stabiliser Cm (m = frameSymmetry, default p). The renderer reaches each tile by the shortest route from the camera, so when the camera crosses into a new tile the routes change and every tile’s frame may change by a rotation of 2πk/m about its own centre. That is a property of the group, not a bug. So tile art must be invariant under rotation by 2π/m, and must not depend on the tile’s word address — word addresses are not canonical, so two routes to one tile can spell it differently. Break either half and the art looks fine standing still and jumps as you scroll.

The default artwork is a pinwheel: one wedge of 2π/m carrying a curved blade and an off-axis dot, repeated m times by exact rotation. That is symmetric to machine precision by construction and as asymmetric as the rule permits — no mirror, no rotation finer than 2π/m — so a mirrored, rotated or misplaced tile still stands out. Select illegal to see what breaking the rule looks like, and colour by address hash to break just the addressing half. The binary tiling is exempt from both: its stabiliser is trivial and its addresses are canonical, which is why it was always the one that scrolled cleanly.

The property to check by eye: a regular tiling is homogeneous, so however far you scroll, the picture should look exactly as it did at the origin. Use the distance buttons to jump hundreds of tiles out and compare. max|V| in the readout is the number that matters underneath: it must stay near 1 forever.
